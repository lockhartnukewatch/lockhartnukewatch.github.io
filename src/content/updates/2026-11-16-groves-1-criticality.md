On August 5, 2026, **Groves-1** — Oklo's Groves Isotope Test Reactor in Lockhart — reached initial criticality. Then, on November 11, the DOE [cleared it for unrestricted operations](https://investor.oklo.com/news/news-details/2026/Oklos-Groves-1-to-Begin-Research-and-Demonstration-Phase-after-being-Cleared-by-U-S--Department-of-Energy-for-Unrestricted-Operations/default.aspx), beginning what Oklo describes as a "research and demonstration phase."

A reactor in active operation is a bigger deal for the community than a reactor in commissioning. The most complete independent accounts of what this clearance means are the American Nuclear Society's [Nuclear News roundup from this week](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-11-16/article-8581/oklo-roundup-groves1-plans-centrus-contract-aurora-concrete/) and [World Nuclear News' coverage of the November 11 clearance](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/us-test-reactor-cleared-for-operations). We dug further into what's actually in the public record about the site itself. (A companion piece covers the separately announced Groves-2 Forge pilot: [Groves-2 Forge: a Lu-177 pilot — but where, and under what review?](/updates/2026-11-16-oklo-groves-2-forge-lu-177/).)

## What the DOE's own environmental document says about Groves-1

The DOE NEPA determination for Groves-1 ([CX-271014](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/CX-271014.pdf)) is the first real, citable description of the site, published June 15, 2026:

- Maximum power **100 watts**, operating phase of **about 100 hours**, planned complete by end of 2026
- **5 Framatome GAIA fuel assemblies**, low-enriched uranium dioxide, and "the reactor won't be refueled"
- A 4,600 sq ft building (1,650 sq ft reactor room) containing a **35-foot-deep, below-grade concrete pool**, on the Proto-Town site in Caldwell County (0.2 mi NW of Mineral Springs Rd, 0.1 mi SW of West Fork Rd), within Oklo's lease of ~47 acres
- Maximum hypothetical accident: a fuel-handling accident with cladding failure; DOE says the dose consequences to workers and public are "minimal"
- Water: ~180,000–200,000 gal/yr potable, **~35,000 gal/yr pool makeup**, a **100,000-gallon static firewater tank**
- Waste: "very small quantities"; **no liquid radioactive sources of concern expected**; contaminated structures shipped to "a commercially operated low-level radioactive waste disposal facility"

Read closely, this document describes a **short-duration, near-zero-power experiment** — not a production plant. It is a categorical exclusion (B5.26) covering *Groves-1 only*, issued for a specific proposed action: fuel possession and operation of that reactor. **Groves-2 is not in it.** The pilot plant Oklo announced on October 13 will need its own DOE authorization and environmental review — and until that document exists, no one, including Oklo's press office, can tell you what the safety basis of Groves-2 actually is. We cover that gap in our [Groves-2 piece](/updates/2026-11-16-oklo-groves-2-forge-lu-177/).

## The construction record doesn't match the environmental review

The state's [TABS record (TABS2026014595)](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/TABS/Projects/TABS2026014595) for the "Groves" project at Proto Town, 2600 Mineral Springs Rd, lists a **7,600 sq ft** single-story industrial facility, "Inspection Complete" as of April 2026, estimated cost $23M, privately funded. Two things to note: the DOE's own NEPA document describes a **4,600 sq ft** building — and the TABS record names the tenant as **Thomas Eiden**, founder of Atomic Alchemy, the isotope company Oklo acquired in February 2025. Either the building grew after the environmental determination was written, or the footprints describe different scopes. We'd like Oklo to explain.

## There's still no NRC record for the reactor

Searches of [NRC ADAMS](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/adams) for the Groves *reactor* still yield nothing, for the same reason as in our last update: the pilot-phase reactor runs under **DOE authorization, not NRC licensing**. Our [FOIA requests](/topics/00-foia-requests/) are still the main channel for the missing reactor design documents. That will start to change only if and when the commercial track for Groves-2 begins — the licensing path Oklo is counting on, and the new October NRC rule that makes it more plausible, are covered in our [Groves-2 piece](/updates/2026-11-16-oklo-groves-2-forge-lu-177/).

## What the November 11 clearance means

Per [Nuclear News](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-11-16/article-8581/oklo-roundup-groves1-plans-centrus-contract-aurora-concrete/), Groves-1 was the **fifth reactor to reach criticality through DOE's Reactor Pilot Program** (one of 11 projects the DOE selected for it in August 2025), and the November 11 clearance makes it what Oklo describes as an "operating platform for research, testing, and training" — explicitly to test capabilities "relevant to future isotope reactors and Oklo's fast fission reactors, the Aurora Powerhouse."

The work has already begun: during a recent startup, operators wore eye-tracking glasses, with the data to be used to "improve the facility's human-machine interface and inform future design choices."

Per [World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/us-test-reactor-cleared-for-operations), the clearance's scope extends beyond Oklo's own roadmap: it includes continued control-rod drive mechanism testing and "criticality and reactivity benchmarking" that Oklo says will help improve fuel performance and power output in **small and large light water reactors** — Groves-1 is, in part, a benchmarking and training asset for the broader light-water fleet, not just a stepping stone for Aurora. Oklo also positions it explicitly as a training and qualification platform for the nuclear personnel who will staff future reactor deployments.

Not every RPP participant keeps its demonstration reactor — fellow participant Antares has fully disassembled its Mark-0. Oklo will keep Groves-1 as an operating research and training facility, presumably for the long term. For the neighborhood, that means a working research reactor is not a temporary experiment: it's a standing presence.

## First responders: still in the dark

And yet the emergency-planning questions from our [May 11 update](/updates/2026-05-11-our-response-to-oklo/) remain unanswered. [Reporting from The Texas Observer, summarized by Magica](https://magica.com/news/oklo-groves-reactor-emergency-planning-lockhart), notes that Oklo **declined to confirm whether it had conducted local emergency drills or would hold a public safety meeting**. That was about a 100-watt test reactor *in commissioning* — now it's operating with unrestricted clearance.

If the announced Groves-2 Forge pilot ever comes to the same backyard, the hazard picture changes entirely — our [Groves-2 piece](/updates/2026-11-16-oklo-groves-2-forge-lu-177/) covers what a continuously operating irradiation facility plus a hot cell of radioactive chemistry would mean for emergency response.

- What is the radiological emergency response plan, and who has it?
- Has the fire department been trained — and with what equipment?
- Who at the site is available 24/7/365 to brief responders?
- What communication protocol do Lockhart residents have, beyond "we will share appropriate public information as the project advances"?

## What we're watching

1. **Status of our FOIA requests** to the DOE — still pending, and still the cleanest path to the reactor design documents Oklo cites as trade secrets.
2. **Emergency planning and first-responder training** — any confirmation of drills, a public safety meeting, or a radiological emergency response plan. The unrestricted-operations clearance makes this more urgent, not less.
3. **What Groves-1 is actually doing** — the cleared work includes control-rod drive testing and reactivity benchmarking. We'll be watching DOE publications and Oklo announcements for what happens inside the reactor next.

Groves-1 started as a modest experiment — 100 watts, a hundred hours, five fuel assemblies, a building whose construction record doesn't quite match its environmental review. Under the latest clearance it becomes a standing research, testing, and training platform: a working reactor in this neighborhood, not a temporary experiment. The name says "Groves." Public records do not really say what happens next.
