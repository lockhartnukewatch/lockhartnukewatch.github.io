---
title: "Oklo's Yb Feedstock: The Actinide Question"
date: 2026-11-19
---

On September 10, 2026, Oklo published a post reporting that [Actinide Inc.](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu) had delivered two grams of enriched ytterbium-176 to its Groves facility in Lockhart ([Oklo](https://oklo.com/blog/two-grams-to-help-build-americas-isotope-supply)).

The post, by Oklo's Head of Isotopes Thomas Eiden, called it "another important piece of Oklo Isotopes' business" and later wrote: "Working with domestic companies like Actinide Inc. is our opportunity to change that" — a line that has quietly become the center of Oklo's publicly hinted isotope-supply story.

For Groves-2 Forge, the isotope production and processing pilot Oklo announced in October, the identity of that supplier is not a minor detail. It is the linchpin of the plan.

## The hinted feedstock chain

Groves-2 Forge is designed to be Oklo's integrated answer to a supply problem: the global supply of ytterbium-176, the stable isotope that feeds the indirect route to lutetium-177, has historically depended heavily on Russia and remains concentrated among relatively few suppliers ([Oklo](https://oklo.com/blog/two-grams-to-help-build-americas-isotope-supply)).

Oklo's pitch: secure enriched Yb-176 feedstock in the U.S., irradiate it in a high-flux reactor to make Lu-177, then chemically separate and package the product. The [Oklo blog](https://oklo.com/blog/two-grams-to-help-build-americas-isotope-supply) describes the two grams delivered from Actinide as "another step toward building a domestic supply chain for isotopes that can support critical medical treatments," and adds: "For isotopes, the irradiation target feedstock is just as important as the steam generator or as the turbine is for a nuclear power facility."

The reactor chemistry is unforgiving. Per a 2021 *European Journal of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging* review, current Lu-177 production runs in reactors with high neutron flux — FRM-II (20 MW), HFR (Netherlands), BR2 (Belgium), MURR (10 MW), and others ([Oklo](https://oklo.com/blog/two-grams-to-help-build-americas-isotope-supply); [European Journal of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4463871/)). The best-documented case of Oklo's exact route is FRM-II, where a 100 mg target of 99.7%-enriched Yb-176 produced up to ~94 GBq of no-carrier-added ([NCA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4463871/)) Lu-177 over a 14-day cycle, and targets up to 150 mg produced up to **120 GBq (~3.2 Ci) per cycle** — enough for a few hundred patient doses.

## Who is Actinide, really?

Actinide, Inc. is an advanced materials company founded in September 2025 by CEO [Eric Olszewski](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ericolszewski) and CTO [Robert Mendelsohn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/robertemendelsohn), which designs, builds, and operates its own isotope-separation machines ([Actinide](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu)).

Its first-generation [calutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calutron), "Endurance," is a modern descendant of the Manhattan Project's Oak Ridge calutrons, rebuilt around superconducting magnets and modern vacuum/controls ([TBPN](https://daily.scuttlebutt.co/p/robert-mendelsohn-and-eric-olszewski)). The company says Endurance first produced enriched ytterbium-176 — more than 95% Yb-176 — delivered to Oklo Isotopes, then was reconfigured to enrich natural uranium into [high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-assay_low-enriched_uranium), producing material assayed at 15.38% uranium-235 under the NRC's laboratory-scale exclusion ([Actinide](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu)).

The HALEU result was research-scale, however, and Actinide acknowledged the material would still need conversion into a form usable by a reactor ([Actinide](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu)).

Its second-generation machine, "Fortitude," is under construction and estimated to deliver roughly half the throughput of the entire U.S. federal electromagnetic-separation fleet in a single unit ([Actinide](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu); [The Tech Pencil](https://thetechpencil.com/article/actinide-isotope-separation-yb-176-haleu)).

On its own telling, Actinide became the first startup in history to enrich uranium and produce HALEU, and says enriched Yb-176 has been delivered commercially to Oklo Isotopes at roughly $30,000 per gram ([Actinide](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu); [TBPN](https://daily.scuttlebutt.co/p/robert-mendelsohn-and-eric-olszewski); [FOX 4](https://www.fox4news.com/news/plano-company-makes-breakthrough-cancer-treatment-material)).

The company raised an oversubscribed seed round in March 2026 led by [Onto Ventures](https://www.onto.vc/portfolio/actinide), with participation from Neo Ventures, Mana Ventures, Discipulus Ventures, Shor Capital, and angel investors ([Actinide](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu)).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claimed / demonstrated** | First-generation calutron delivered enriched Yb-176 (>95% Yb-176) to Oklo; HALEU result at research scale (15.38%), under NRC lab exemption; Fortitude (2nd gen) under construction |
| **Scale** | Grams of Yb-176 delivered; commercial deliveries at ~$30k/gram ([Actinide](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu); [TBPN](https://daily.scuttlebutt.co/p/robert-mendelsohn-and-eric-olszewski)); kg-scale production unproven |

## The Oklo–Actinide link

The relationship is now clearer than Oklo's own communications sometimes let on. Oklo [acquired Atomic Alchemy](https://www.powermag.com/oklos-25m-move-into-radioisotope-production-to-expand-advanced-nuclear-revenue-stream/) in February 2025 for $25 million in all-stock, adding radioisotope production capabilities to Oklo's reactor and fuel-recycling operations. Atomic Alchemy was founded in 2018 by [Thomas Eiden](https://twitter.com/AtomicEiden) (X; profile archived at [TwStalker](https://twstalker.com/AtomicEiden)), whom Oklo then brought in as its Senior Director of Isotopes; the TwStalker profile describes him as "Founder & CEO of Atomic Alchemy" and "Senior Director of Isotopes at Oklo" ([TwStalker](https://twstalker.com/AtomicEiden)).

The Groves-1 building at Proto-Town, Oklo's lease site in Caldwell County, is registered in TCEQ/Texas records under the tenant name **Thomas Eiden / Atomic Alchemy** ([Groves-1 piece](/updates/2026-11-16-groves-1-criticality/)).

So the hinted chain reads: **Actinide (enrichment) → Oklo Isotopes (irradiation + chemical processing) → Lu-177 → cancer treatment.** Oklo Isotopes is the customer; Atomic Alchemy — now an Oklo subsidiary — is the bridge; Actinide is the upstream producer.

## Can Actinide deliver the feedstock Groves-2 needs?

Groves-2 Forge is supposed to be the machine that turns that feedstock into product: a reactor plus chemical plant, with "irradiation, processing, and product finishing" brought together ([Oklo](https://oklo.com/newsroom/oklo-to-advance-groves-2-forge-as-part-of-u-s-department-of-energys-nuclear-energy-launch-pad-se)).

The numbers Oklo has published point at a high-flux production reactor:

- Groves-1, the test reactor Oklo has operated, was capped at **100 watts** by its DOE determination, with an operating phase of ~100 hours ([Groves-1 piece](/updates/2026-11-16-groves-1-criticality/)).
- At FRM-II-like flux, 100 mg of enriched Yb-176 → ~94 GBq of NCA Lu-177 per cycle. Groves-2's disclosed feedstock is two grams — roughly **1.5 TBq (~40 Ci) per cycle, a few hundred patient doses**.
- Commercial NCA Lu-177 production runs on **kilograms of enriched Yb-176 per year**. Two grams is pilot quantity.
- Groves-2's reactor in Oklo's own [Fall 2026 Groves teach-in deck](https://s203.q4cdn.com/103172959/files/doc_presentations/2026/Sep/30/Fall-2026-Groves-Teach-in-vF.pdf) is still marked **"WORKING DRAFT"**, describes a "compact" reactor, "multiple reactors per facility," and 100% capacity allocated to isotope production.

The scale gap is the crux: Groves-2 must be orders of magnitude (perhaps hundreds of thousands times) more powerful than Groves-1, and Actinide's demonstrated capability so far is a first-generation calutron that has delivered **grams**, not kilograms.

## How likely is Actinide to fulfill the required feedstock?

As of the latest public record in November 2026, the answer is: **unlikely to be the near-term supplier of record for Groves-2 Forge's commercial feedstock.** Actinide's path runs through its first-generation calutron and its unproven second-generation Fortitude; its HALEU demonstration was research-scale and under the NRC laboratory exemption; and the public NRC material as of September 2026 does not list Actinide among enrichment applicants ([The Tech Pencil](https://thetechpencil.com/article/actinide-isotope-separation-yb-176-haleu); [Actinide](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu)).

The only end-to-end domestic producer of enriched Yb-176 today is the federal government — Oak Ridge National Laboratory's National Isotope Development Center, which enriches Yb-176 while collecting Yb-171 in the same run ([The Tech Pencil](https://thetechpencil.com/article/actinide-isotope-separation-yb-176-haleu)).

## Sourcing the feedstock from Oak Ridge?

Beyond the Actinide thread, Oklo has signaled that Oak Ridge, Tennessee — home to the Department of Energy's National Isotope Development Center and the company's planned Advanced Fuel Center — is a second arrow in its quiver. Oklo's Q3 2026 materials said it was pursuing "enrichment partnerships and recycling used nuclear fuel through its planned Advanced Fuel Center in Oak Ridge, Tennessee," and was seeking surplus government plutonium for later reactors ([TradingNEWS](https://www.tradingnews.com/news/oklo-eyes-30-usd-after-38-percent-2-day-rally-as-3b-usd-cash-pile-offset-aurora-28-29-start-up-slip); [Oklo 10-Q](https://www.publicnow.com/view/AFB2B4AFCFB392E3C02BB4886B1F6D4EDCC45F80)).

Oak Ridge's electromagnetic separation already enriches Yb-176 for the Lu-177 precursor route, collecting Yb-171 in the same run ([The Tech Pencil](https://thetechpencil.com/article/actinide-isotope-separation-yb-176-haleu)). Whether Oklo would source Yb-176 from Oak Ridge directly, or bid alongside other DOE/National Lab customers (some of which could be competitors in this space) for access to that capacity, is not public — but Oak Ridge is now a named part of Oklo's disclosed feedstock strategy.

## Risks to Oklo's publicly disclosed business plans

If Actinide is the supplier Oklo is counting on, then the entire isotope-revenue narrative is contingent on a single startup's unproven scale-up. Oklo's own disclosures support the skepticism:

- Oklo's Q3 2026 results said first isotope revenue would come **early 2027, from the NRC-licensed Idaho radiochemistry laboratory, not from Texas** ([TradingNEWS](https://www.tradingnews.com/news/oklo-eyes-30-usd-after-38-percent-2-day-rally-as-3b-usd-cash-pile-offset-aurora-28-29-start-up-slip)).
- The Q3 revenue of **$1.86M** was entirely from Oklo's acquired engineering businesses; none came from power, fuel, or isotopes ([TradingNEWS](https://www.tradingnews.com/news/oklo-eyes-30-usd-after-38-percent-2-day-rally-as-3b-usd-cash-pile-offset-aurora-28-29-start-up-slip)).
- The company reported a **$123.3M net loss** through nine months of 2026, with **$110.9M** in operating cash use, and held **$3.131B** in liquidity against an estimated **$6.5B** in remaining funding needs through 2030 ([The Inference](https://theinference.org/article/oklo-s-cancer-isotope-plant-lands-with-no-buyer-no-price-and-a-6-5-billion-bill)).
- The DOE's October 13 Launch Pad announcement named **no contract value, no customer, no production volume, and no federal dollar amount** ([The Inference](https://theinference.org/article/oklo-s-cancer-isotope-plant-lands-with-no-buyer-no-price-and-a-6-5-billion-bill)).

## Missing links

To date, we haven't found evidence that would fill in these gaps:

1. **Feedstock.** Oklo's disclosed Yb-176 inventory is two grams. The producer capable of delivering the kilogram-scale feedstock commercial Lu-177 production requires is not demonstrated.
2. **Reactor.** Groves-2 Forge's power level is undisclosed; the only public reactor design that plausibly runs this chemistry is a 10–20 MW high-flux research reactor, and Oklo's deck for Groves-2 is a "working draft."
3. **Licensing.** The DOE NEPA determination for Groves-2 does not yet exist; no NRC docket for commercial operation has been filed; Groves-2 construction is targeted to begin within 12 months at a site that is "still being finalized."

## The safety angle

The feedstock, reactor, licensing, and waste gaps above are also safety gaps — and the safety framework under which a facility like Groves-2 Forge will operate is itself moving faster than the public conversation about it.

### The nuclear safety rules are being rewritten

In light of the [Department of Energy's quiet rewriting of nuclear safety rules](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/28/nx-s1-5677187/nuclear-safety-rules-rewritten-trump) (NPR), the pace at which Oklo has moved from Groves-1's first criticality to the planned Groves-2 Forge takes on a sharper safety meaning (NPR). According to the NPR article, the DOE cut more than 750 pages of requirements covering safety systems, environmental protection, site security, and accident investigation — leaving about a third of the original pages — and removed the "As Low As Reasonably Achievable" principle, the cognizant system engineer requirement, and softened radiation-protection language from mandatory to discretionary.

A [follow-up](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/26/nx-s1-5727510/secret-rules-experimental-nuclear-reactors-now-public) from NPR (Feb. 26, 2026) made the rewritten orders public in response to a FOIA request, and [ProPublica's](https://www.propublica.org/article/trump-nuclear-power-nrc-safety-doge-vought) investigation (March 20, 2026) found that the DOE's radioactive waste management manual — 59 pages — had been replaced by a 25-page order in which pages of detailed requirements for waste packaging and monitoring had been removed.

The [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/16/2026-14341/modernizing-reactor-licensing-safety-oversight-and-siting-practices) proposed rule of July 16, 2026 and the NRC's September 24, 2026 proposed enhancements are the institutional expression of this rollback.

Independent reviewers have warned that the net effect was a loosening. Former NRC chair Christopher Hanson said the secret rewriting ["is not the best way to engender the kind of public trust that's going to be needed for nuclear to succeed more broadly"](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/28/nx-s1-5677187/nuclear-safety-rules-rewritten-trump#:~:text=%22I%20would,broadly%2C%22); Tison Campbell warned that removing the ALARA principle could mean [less concrete shielding and longer shifts](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/28/nx-s1-5677187/nuclear-safety-rules-rewritten-trump#:~:text=Removing%20the%20standard%20means%20that%20new%20reactors%20could%20be%20constructed%20with%20less%20concrete%20shielding%2C%20and%20workers%20could%20work%20longer%20shifts%2C%20potentially%20receiving%20higher%20doses%20of%20radiation); Emily Caffrey said the shift from mandatory ("prohibited" or "must") to discretionary ("should be" or "can be") language was ["a big change in words, in meaning."](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/28/nx-s1-5677187/nuclear-safety-rules-rewritten-trump#:~:text=%22Anywhere%20they%20have%20changed%20%27prohibited%27%20or%20%27must%27%20to%20%27should%20be%27%20or%20%27can%20be%27%20%E2%80%94%20that%20is%20a%20loosening%20of%20regulation%2E%20That%27s%20a%20big%20change%20in%20words%2C%20in%20meaning%2C%22%20Caffrey%20said)

### What kinds of dangerous waste might be onsite — and how much?

The most dangerous material at a facility like Groves-2 Forge would be the by-products of the fission reaction used to irradiate the isotope materials, and the used fuel that remains in the reactor system until relocation.

The Yb-176 → Lu-177 isotope reaction does not produce plutonium or tritium, but the HALEU-powered reactor that serves as the neutron source might. Oklo has not disclosed the design of Groves-2 or what waste products the Forge reactor might product, or in what quantities. With the project under DOE, rather than NRC oversight, these details are not available to the public.

Certainly a larger scale reactor will require safeguards, procedures and processes which cannot be kept secret. First responders need to be trained in order to keep themselves and the community surrounding the site safe, wherever it may end up.

## What we're watching

1. **Fortitude** — independent validation of sustained production, purity, and cost.
2. **The DOE NEPA determination for Groves-2** — the first public statement of the pilot reactor's power, fuel, cooling, and waste.
3. **TCEQ / DSHS** — any new permits, waste characterization, and exemption concurrences for the processing facility.
4. **First contracted isotope delivery** — now expected in 2027, not 2026.
