What Can Oklo's Isotope Supply Chain Deliver?

On September 10, 2026, Oklo published a post reporting that Actinide Inc. had delivered two grams of enriched ytterbium-176 to its Groves facility in Lockhart (Oklo).

The post, by Oklo’s Head of Isotopes Thomas Eiden, called it “another important piece of Oklo Isotopes’ business” and later wrote: “Working with domestic companies like Actinide Inc. is our opportunity to change that” – a line that has quietly become the center of Oklo’s publicly hinted isotope-supply story.

For Groves-2 Forge, the isotope production and processing pilot Oklo announced in October, the identity of that supplier is not a minor detail. It is the linchpin of the plan.

Most medical treatments that rely on radioactive material depend on a very particular kind of nuclear supply chain. One of the most important building blocks is lutetium-177 (Lu-177), a radioactive isotope that emits beta particles to kill cancer cells, used in targeted therapies such as Pluvicto and Lutathera.

To make Lu-177, you start with a stable isotope – ytterbium-176, written Yb-176 and pronounced “ytterbium-176” – and put it in a nuclear reactor, where it captures a neutron and decays into lutetium-177. The entire chain – enrich, irradiate, separate, package – is enormously complex, and for most of the nuclear age the global supply of enriched ytterbium-176 has depended heavily on Russia.

The hinted feedstock chain

Groves-2 Forge is designed to be Oklo’s integrated answer to a supply problem: the global supply of ytterbium-176, the stable isotope that feeds the indirect route to lutetium-177, has historically depended heavily on Russia and remains concentrated among relatively few suppliers (Oklo).

Oklo’s pitch: secure enriched Yb-176 feedstock in the U.S., irradiate it in a high-flux reactor to make Lu-177, then chemically separate and package the product. The Oklo blog describes the two grams delivered from Actinide as “another step toward building a domestic supply chain for isotopes that can support critical medical treatments,” and adds: “For isotopes, the irradiation target feedstock is just as important as the steam generator or as the turbine is for a nuclear power facility.”

The chemistry points to hard energy requirements for the neutron source. Per a 2021 European Journal of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging review, current Lu-177 production runs in reactors with high neutron flux – FRM-II (20 MW), HFR (Netherlands), BR2 (Belgium), MURR (10 MW), and others (Oklo; European Journal of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging). The best-documented case of Oklo’s exact route is FRM-II, where a 100 mg target of 99.7%-enriched Yb-176 produced up to ~94 GBq of no-carrier-added (NCA) Lu-177 over a 14-day cycle, and targets up to 150 mg produced up to 120 GBq (~3.2 Ci) per cycle – enough for a few hundred patient doses.

Who is Actinide, really?

Actinide, Inc. is an advanced materials company founded in September 2025 by CEO Eric Olszewski and CTO Robert Mendelsohn, which designs, builds, and operates its own isotope-separation machines (Actinide).

Its first-generation calutron, “Endurance,” is a modern descendant of the Manhattan Project’s Oak Ridge calutrons, rebuilt around superconducting magnets and modern vacuum/controls (TBPN). The company says Endurance first produced enriched ytterbium-176 – more than 95% Yb-176 – delivered to Oklo Isotopes, then was reconfigured to enrich natural uranium into high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU), producing material assayed at 15.38% uranium-235 under the NRC’s laboratory-scale exclusion (Actinide).

The HALEU result was research-scale, however, and Actinide acknowledged the material would still need conversion into a form usable by a reactor (Actinide).

Its second-generation machine, “Fortitude,” is under construction and estimated to deliver roughly half the throughput of the entire U.S. federal electromagnetic-separation fleet in a single unit (Actinide; The Tech Pencil).

On its own telling, Actinide became the first startup in history to enrich uranium and produce HALEU, and says enriched Yb-176 has been delivered commercially to Oklo Isotopes at roughly $30,000 per gram (Actinide; TBPN; FOX 4).

The company raised an oversubscribed seed round in March 2026 led by Onto Ventures, with participation from Neo Ventures, Mana Ventures, Discipulus Ventures, Shor Capital, and angel investors (Actinide).
Claimed / demonstrated First-generation calutron delivered enriched Yb-176 (>95% Yb-176) to Oklo; HALEU result at research scale (15.38%), under NRC lab exemption; Fortitude (2nd gen) under construction

Can Actinide deliver the feedstock Groves-2 needs?

Groves-2 Forge is supposed to be the machine that turns that feedstock into product: a reactor plus chemical plant, with “irradiation, processing, and product finishing” brought together (Oklo).

The numbers Oklo has published point at a high-flux production reactor:

  • Groves-1, the test reactor Oklo has operated, was capped at 100 watts by its DOE determination, with an operating phase of ~100 hours (Groves-1 piece).
  • At FRM-II-like flux, 100 mg of enriched Yb-176 → ~94 GBq of NCA Lu-177 per cycle. Groves-2’s disclosed feedstock is two grams – roughly 1.5 TBq (~40 Ci) per cycle, a few hundred patient doses.
  • Commercial NCA Lu-177 production runs on kilograms of enriched Yb-176 per year. Two grams is pilot quantity. The Lu-177 Capacity Number Nobody Publishes – ProGen Search’s Aug. 2026 analysis reports that TMC Group produced more than two kilograms of elemental Yb-176 in 2025, and its mass-to-clinical-output bridge implies the commercial feedstock scale is on the order of kilograms per year.
  • Groves-2’s reactor in Oklo’s own Fall 2026 Groves teach-in deck is still marked “WORKING DRAFT”, describes a “compact” reactor, “multiple reactors per facility,” and 100% capacity allocated to isotope production.

The scale gap is the crux: Groves-2 must be orders of magnitude (perhaps hundreds of thousands times) more powerful than Groves-1, and Actinide’s demonstrated capability so far is a first-generation calutron that has delivered grams, not kilograms.

How likely is Actinide to fulfill the required feedstock?

As of the latest public record in November 2026, the answer is: unlikely to be the near-term supplier of record for Groves-2 Forge’s commercial feedstock. Actinide’s path runs through its first-generation calutron and its unproven second-generation Fortitude; its HALEU demonstration was research-scale and under the NRC laboratory exemption; and the public NRC material as of September 2026 does not list Actinide among enrichment applicants (The Tech Pencil; Actinide).

The only end-to-end domestic producer of enriched Yb-176 today is the federal government – Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s National Isotope Development Center, which enriches Yb-176 while collecting Yb-171 in the same run (The Tech Pencil).

Sourcing the feedstock from Oak Ridge?

Beyond the Actinide thread, Oklo has signaled that Oak Ridge, Tennessee – home to the Department of Energy’s National Isotope Development Center and the company’s planned Advanced Fuel Center – is a second arrow in its quiver. Oklo’s Q3 2026 materials said it was pursuing “enrichment partnerships and recycling used nuclear fuel through its planned Advanced Fuel Center in Oak Ridge, Tennessee,” and was seeking surplus government plutonium for later reactors (TradingNEWS; Oklo 10-Q).

Oak Ridge’s electromagnetic separation already enriches Yb-176 for the Lu-177 precursor route, collecting Yb-171 in the same run (The Tech Pencil). Whether Oklo would source Yb-176 from Oak Ridge directly, or bid alongside other DOE/National Lab customers (some of which could be competitors in this space) for access to that capacity, is not public – but Oak Ridge is now a named part of Oklo’s disclosed feedstock strategy.

Risks to Oklo’s publicly disclosed business plans

If Actinide is the supplier Oklo is counting on, then the entire isotope-revenue narrative is contingent on a single startup’s unproven scale-up. Oklo’s own disclosures support the skepticism:

  • Oklo’s Q3 2026 results said first isotope revenue would come early 2027, from the NRC-licensed Idaho radiochemistry laboratory, not from Texas (TradingNEWS).
  • The Q3 revenue of $1.86M was entirely from Oklo’s acquired engineering businesses; none came from power, fuel, or isotopes (TradingNEWS).
  • Oklo reported a $123.3M net loss through nine months of 2026, with $110.9M in operating cash use, and held $3.131B in liquidity against an estimated $6.5B in remaining funding needs through 2030 (The Inference).
  • The DOE’s October 13 Launch Pad announcement named no contract value, no customer, no production volume, and no federal dollar amount (The Inference).

Missing links

To date, we haven’t found evidence that would fill in these gaps:

  1. Feedstock. Oklo’s disclosed Yb-176 inventory is two grams. The producer capable of delivering the kilogram-scale feedstock commercial Lu-177 production requires is not demonstrated.
  2. Reactor. Groves-2 Forge’s power level is undisclosed; the only public reactor design that plausibly runs this chemistry is a 10–20 MW high-flux research reactor, and Oklo’s deck for Groves-2 is a “working draft.”
  3. Licensing. The DOE NEPA determination for Groves-2 does not yet exist; no NRC docket for commercial operation has been filed; Groves-2 construction is targeted to begin within 12 months at a site that is “still being finalized.”

What we’re watching

  1. Fortitude – independent validation of sustained production, purity, and cost.
  2. The DOE NEPA determination for Groves-2 – the first public statement of the pilot reactor’s power, fuel, cooling, and waste.
  3. TCEQ / DSHS – any new permits, waste characterization, and exemption concurrences for the processing facility.
  4. First contracted isotope delivery – now expected in 2027, not 2026.
